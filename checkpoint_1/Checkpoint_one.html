Accessibility keeps websites compliant with WCAG Tips for alt text * Keep it short: 125 words * Include important details and get to the point quickly * Use neutral, clear language; be mindful of subjective adjectives like funny * Think about the purpose of the image and why it’s there * Don’t mention type of image; just write what it is * check spelling grammar Image description: A pair of trendy sneakers sit on a white podium in front of a white background. Context: This image is being used to showcase a new product from a fashion designer. Ineffective alt text: A pair of trendy sneakers, sitting on a white podium in front of a white background While factually accurate Acceptable alt text: A pair of colorful basketball sneakers with pink laces. Each sneaker has white soles and a beige canvas strap on the side. Optimal alt text: Leather basketball sneakers with purple, yellow, grey, and green detailing, chunky white rubber soles, and red laces When it comes to graphs and charts, alt text should summarize what the information is about. For banners, it should repeat verbatim what is said. Unless it's a heading followed by a list, there should be a separate text description underneath the graphic, and it should be a concise summary If an image is decorative or already described, it doesn’t need alt text. Use the null attribute (i.e., alt=””) so the screen reader knows to move past it. A website's Information Architecture has two main components: identification and definition of the site’s content and functionality. IA is not a part of UI; it informs UI. IA is made in a spreadsheet with comprehensive layouts known as comps or prototypes. Activities included in defining IA is * Content inventory- looking at a website to locate and identify existing site content * Content audit- Evaluation of content usefulness, accuracy, tone of voice, and overall effectiveness * Information grouping-Definition of user-centered relationships between content * Taxonomy development- Definition of a standardized naming convention (controlled vocabulary) to apply to all site content * Descriptive information creation-Definition of useful metadata that can be utilized to generate “Related Link” lists or other navigation components that aid discovery Website navigation is a collection of UI that helps users find information, functionality, and point them in the direction you want to go. Decisions to think about when doing navigation * Usage Priority: How much will users rely on this navigation component? * Placement: On which pages should it be present? Where should it be placed within the page layout grid? * Pattern: Which navigation design patterns best support findability and discoverability? You can’t ignore IA and only focus on navigation because navigation doesn’t tell you the full scope of you websites needs. The IA doesn’t have to be complete, too. move forward, but you have to do it first to give you an idea of what you’re doing.
